The story

Chalchitra Play is a mid-sized Indian streaming app: regional films, web series, a growing library in nine languages, plans from ₹149 a month. Between January 2024 and March 2026 it signed up 15,000 subscribers through ads, Instagram, referrals and a telco bundle. Close to half of them have already left.

Nobody slams the door. Subscribers watch a little less each month, skip a weekend, and then simply do not renew. Others never meant to leave at all: their card expired or their UPI mandate hit its limit, the renewal failed twice, and the account closed quietly. The growth team calls this group the quiet quitters, and it wants a model that finds them before they go, so it can call them with a retention offer.

The data team has handed over four exports taken on 31 March 2026. They look generous: the customer table even has a health score, days since last login and a cancellation reason. That is the trap. Half of those columns were written after the customer left, and a model that uses them will look brilliant in a notebook and fail on the first Monday in production.

Your job is to build the model properly: an honest population, a label that respects time, features that only use the past, and a test on a window the model has never seen. Then turn the scores into a call list and a rupee number the head of growth can act on. Your license shares a business brief with four other buyers: your own segment, offer cost, save rate and call capacity.
