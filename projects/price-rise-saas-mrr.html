What is messy in this dataset?

These are the problems you will find and fix before any analysis. They are put in on purpose, because real exports look like this.

  • 137 billing events sent twice by webhook retries, and the file is not in time order
  • Event times in ISO, +00:00 style and Unix seconds
  • Amounts in cents on some rows and dollars on others; yearly plans stored as the yearly amount
  • Event types written three ways each (customer.subscription.deleted, canceled, cancelled)
  • 28 internal test accounts mixed in with customers
  • Account ids as ACC-00123, acc-00123, ACC00123

What business problem does this project solve?

A SaaS product analytics project: two years of a B2B app's billing events, invoices and usage. Build MRR, the MRR bridge, churn and cohort retention, then decide if a price rise paid off.

Inkwell is a docs-and-notes tool for small teams, sold by the seat. Customers in more than a dozen countries pay monthly or yearly, on three plans: Starter, Pro and Team.

On 1 March 2025 the company raised prices: Starter from $12 to $15 a seat, Pro from $25 to $29. Team stayed at $49. New customers paid the new price straight away. Existing monthly customers moved to it at their first renewal from 1 May, and yearly customers at their next yearly renewal.

The CEO says the price rise worked: monthly revenue went up. The head of customer success says it didn't: she saw a wave of cancellations in May and June. Both are looking at different dashboards, and neither trusts the other's numbers.

The data comes straight from the billing system. Its event export has webhook retries (the same event twice), three time formats including Unix seconds, amounts in cents on some rows and dollars on others, yearly plans stored as the yearly amount, and a couple of dozen internal test accounts mixed in with real customers. Invoices and monthly usage come from other systems with their own quirks.

Your job is to build the numbers everyone will use from now on: MRR at every month end, the MRR bridge (new, expansion, contraction, churn, reactivation), churn, retention by cohort, and a straight answer to the question that started the argument. Was the price rise worth it?

Every row is synthetic, built to behave like real SaaS billing data. Your licence gives you your own signup cohort and your own plan to report on.
