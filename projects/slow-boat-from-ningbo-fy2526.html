What is messy in this dataset?

These are the problems you will find and fix before any analysis. They are put in on purpose, because real exports look like this.

  • Purchase orders revised as 260411, 260411.R, 260411.R.1; only the latest counts
  • Up to seven spellings per supplier factory; ports written four ways
  • Money in USD and INR text in six formats; five date formats including Excel serials
  • Contact name, email and phone crammed into one cell in four layouts
  • Product code blank on about 6% and line value blank on about 7% of rows

What business problem does this project solve?

Track 470 China import POs across six messy files: containers on water, overdue documents, pending payments and the cash plan, as of your own report date.

Kaveri Home Appliances is a Noida based brand that sells air fryers, vacuum cleaners, mixers, kettles and a dozen other small appliances across India. It does not own a factory. Every product is made by one of 14 factories in Zhejiang and Guangdong, shipped from Ningbo, Nansha or Shekou, and cleared at ICD Tughlakabad, Mundra, Nhava Sheva or Chennai.

You have just joined as the procurement analyst. The import desk runs on one shared spreadsheet, the PO register, plus whatever the freight forwarder emails, whatever the shipping line's website says, a documents tracker that the CHA updates when he remembers, and a payments sheet that accounts keeps separately. Between April 2025 and June 2026 the company placed 470 purchase orders worth about USD 21.7 million. 92 of those POs were revised at least once, and the register keeps every version.

The head of supply chain has three questions every Monday morning: where is every container right now, which documents is someone waiting on, and how much cash does finance need to arrange for suppliers, freight and customs in the coming weeks. Nobody can answer them without an afternoon of copying and pasting. The register's own Revised ETA column says most shipments are on time. The warehouse disagrees. And the demurrage bills from the shipping lines keep getting bigger, with nobody quite sure why.

You have six exports and a report date of your own. Your job is to join the files into one trustworthy model, rebuild the world as it looked on your report date, and give the head of supply chain the tracker, the documents chase list and the cash plan they have never had. Then find out why the containers sit at the port.

What makes this dataset different: your report date and your focus supplier are personal to your license, so your tracker, your overdue documents and your cash plan are yours alone. No two buyers have the same answer key.
