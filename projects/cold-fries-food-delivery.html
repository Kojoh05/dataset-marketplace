What is messy in this dataset?

These are the problems you will find and fix before any analysis. They are put in on purpose, because real exports look like this.

  • Three clocks: UTC, local time with no zone, and Unix epoch milliseconds
  • Daylight saving ends on 26 October in London and 2 November in Chicago
  • Chicago distances in miles, the rest in km; money in $, £ and S$
  • About 1,800 blank Ready times and 3,400 pressed after the courier left
  • 594 duplicate orders and 870 duplicate courier events
  • City codes CHI, LDN, SG and three local date styles

What business problem does this project solve?

An operations analytics project: four months of food delivery orders in Chicago, London and Singapore, logged on three different clocks. Build every order's timeline and find where the late minutes go.

Dashly is a food delivery app in three cities: Chicago, London and Singapore. Customers see a promised delivery time when they order. About one order in five arrives later than promised, and the refunds for late and cold food are growing.

Each city's operations lead has a theory. Chicago blames the restaurants for slow kitchens. London blames rain and traffic. Singapore thinks the new batching feature, where one courier carries two orders, is making things worse. The head of operations wants one answer built on the same numbers for all three cities: where do the delivery minutes actually go?

The trouble is time itself. The app logs order times in UTC. Restaurant tablets log local time with no time zone. Courier phones log Unix epoch milliseconds. Delivered times are local and written the way each city writes dates, and the clocks changed in London on 26 October and in Chicago on 2 November, so a fixed offset is wrong for half the data. Chicago measures distance in miles. Some restaurants press the Ready button after the courier has already left. Some orders and courier events were exported twice.

Your job: put every timestamp on one clock, build a timeline for every order (accept, assign, courier to restaurant, prep, wait, travel), find what makes orders late in each city, and tell the head of operations the three fixes worth making first.

Every row is synthetic, built to behave like real delivery data. Your licence gives you your own city and your own week to report on.
