The story

Raahi Express is a regional last-mile courier serving the small cities and towns of Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Jharkhand and Madhya Pradesh. Twelve destination hubs (Lucknow, Kanpur, Varanasi, Gorakhpur, Prayagraj, Agra, Bareilly, Patna, Muzaffarpur, Gaya, Ranchi and Bhopal) each run four riders and serve a hub city plus the towns around it. Six shippers feed the network: a large marketplace, two D2C brands, a pharmacy, an apparel seller and a hardware wholesaler. Parcels are picked up in Delhi, Kolkata or Mumbai, line-hauled to the destination hub, and then attempted by the rider up to three times before they are returned to the shipper.

Every shipment leaves a trail in the dispatch system: pickup, the promised delivery date, hub arrival, each attempt, the final outcome, what was billed and what the journey cost.

The financial year just closed on 31 March 2026, and the largest shipper, Apnabazaar, has put the contract up for review.

The Operations Head is confident. His dashboard says 92.3% of shipments are delivered on time, and he wants to name Mohit Tiwari of the Lucknow hub "Rider of the Year". The rider handled more parcels than anyone in the network (735) and his first attempts are almost always made on or before the promised date (98.9%, the best of 48 riders).

Apnabazaar's own audit tells a different story. Counting only parcels that actually reached the customer by the promised date, they put Raahi Express at roughly 79.2%. The Finance Head, reading the same export, has found that the Rider of the Year's customers escalate more than five times as often as everyone else's, and that his shipments earn less than the network average (₹8 each against ₹15).

Nobody agrees which number is right, why parcels are late, or whether the network is making any money on the work it wins. The shipper wants an answer before next peak season.
