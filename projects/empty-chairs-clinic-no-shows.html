What is messy in this dataset?

These are the problems you will find and fix before any analysis. They are put in on purpose, because real exports look like this.

  • Four leaky columns: check-in time, visit minutes, co-pay collected and a total no-shows count that includes the future
  • Three time formats, including month-first with AM/PM
  • No-shows written as No Show, DNA, noshow
  • Birth years of 1900 and 0, distances of -1 and 9999
  • About 7% of reminders sent after the scoring moment
  • Specialty and clinic names that change between systems

What business problem does this project solve?

A data science project: predict empty chairs at six outpatient clinics from two years of messy appointments, patients and reminders. Avoid four leaks, validate on unseen months, and price an overbooking rule.

Harbor Health runs six outpatient clinics: general practice, dermatology, physiotherapy, cardiology, pediatrics, mental health and orthopedics. Last year about one booked appointment in six ended with an empty chair. The patient did not come, or cancelled so late that nobody else could take the slot.

Every empty chair costs money: a clinician paid to wait, and another patient who waited weeks for a slot that went unused. The operations director wants a model that scores each appointment at 6 pm two days before it, so the booking team can call the risky ones, and overbook a few slots where the risk is high enough.

She has been burned before. A vendor showed her a model with almost perfect accuracy that fell apart in real use. It had learned from columns that are only filled in after the appointment: the check-in time, the visit length, whether a co-pay was collected, and a "total no-shows" count on the patient record that includes the future.

The exports are messy too. Three time formats, statuses written five ways (DNA means did not attend), clinic and specialty names that change between systems, birth years of 1900 and 0, distances of 9999. Reminders are logged separately, and some were sent after the scoring moment.

Your job: define the population and the label honestly, build features that were known at 6 pm two days before, validate on the last three months the model has never seen, and turn the scores into an overbooking rule worth money for your clinic.

Every row is synthetic, built to behave like real clinic data. Your licence gives you a clinic and its costs, shared with four other buyers.
