The story

Navya Appliances sells mixer grinders, water heaters, air fryers, induction cooktops, chimneys and RO purifiers across India. When one breaks, the customer calls the helpline, a ticket goes to one of 53 authorised service centres (ASCs), and an engineer visits. About half the time the engineer needs a spare part, which has to come from the central warehouse in Noida.

The service dashboard says the average ticket closes in a few days and most are within SLA. Customers say otherwise. Ratings are falling, the same people keep calling back, and every winter the water heater complaints pile up for weeks.

You have the ticket export (17,447 rows), the spare order log (9,221 orders), and the ASC and parts masters for April 2025 to March 2026. The tickets are messy, some ASCs close cases before the product is fixed so the customer has to call again, and the warehouse quietly runs out of the same three parts every season.

Your job is to rebuild the true turnaround time, find where the days really go (the warehouse, the courier or the ASC) and tell the head of service what to fix first. Your license shares a brief with four other buyers: a region and an SLA target.

No. Every row is synthetic: the company, the people and the transactions are invented. The data is built to behave like real business data, including the mess, so the project feels like real work.
