What is messy in this dataset?

These are the problems you will find and fix before any analysis. They are put in on purpose, because real exports look like this.

  • About 190 deals entered twice with the same Deal ID
  • Booking dates in five formats including Excel serials and month names only
  • Each of 56 salespeople under about 8 name spellings
  • Employee codes blank on about 5%, some lower case, some mistyped (EMP01023)
  • Money stored as text in several formats

What business problem does this project solve?

A year of hand-typed ERP entries from an 8-branch car dealer group in Delhi. Clean it, then settle the argument management can't: who really performed, what December's record month actually cost, and why the customers who refused last year's stock were the most profitable buyers of the year.

Sanchay Motors is an authorised Tata Motors dealer group operating eight showrooms across Delhi NCT: Rohini, Dwarka, Pitampura, Karol Bagh, Janakpuri, Lajpat Nagar, Mayur Vihar and Okhla. Fifty-six salespeople work the floor between them, selling the full Tata range: Tiago and Tigor at the entry end, Punch and Nexon in the volume middle, Altroz and Curvv alongside them, Harrier and Safari at the top, the newly launched Sierra from November, and the growing electric line of Tiago EV, Tigor EV, Punch EV, Nexon EV, Curvv EV and Harrier EV.

Every deal gets typed into the dealership's ERP by the salesperson who handled it: the enquiry, the booking, the car, the colour, the price it closed at, how the customer paid, when it was promised and when it actually went out of the gate.

The financial year just closed on 31 March 2026, and the management committee is deadlocked.

The Sales Head wants to hand "Salesperson of the Year" to the person who moved the most metal: 307 cars in twelve months, more than anyone else in the group by a clear margin. Simple, visible, hard to argue with.

The Finance Head has pulled a different number and is refusing to sign off. Every car in the system has a floor price, the lowest the branch is permitted to accept, and a quoted price the salesperson opens at. The gap between the floor and what the customer actually pays is the only money the dealership keeps. By that measure, the Sales Head's champion ranks fiftieth out of fifty-six. He sold the most cars in Delhi and brought home less margin than people who sold a third as many.

Meanwhile there is someone in Karol Bagh who sold only 139 cars all year and earned nearly five times the margin of the volume leader, and someone in Rohini who did both at once. Nobody in the room can agree on what "best" means, and ₹176 lakh of negotiated margin sits at the top of the table with two names within ₹50,000 of each other.

  1. Phase 1, Python: Clean the DataExample task: Load the raw CSV/XLSX and profile it: row count, column count, null counts per column, and a written note on the five things that look most wrong before you touch anything.
  2. Phase 2, SQL: Answer the Business QuestionsExample task: Rank the 56 salespeople by number of Closed deals. Return name, branch, deals closed and their rank.
  3. Phase 3a, Dashboard Page 1: Deal LookupExample task: Build a search box that takes a Deal ID and returns everything about that one deal on a single screen: salesperson + branch, car (model/variant/tier/fuel/colour/model year), the full price chain (ex-showroom → floor → quoted → discount → final), payment/finance detail, and the delivery timeline (enquiry → booking → scheduled → actual, with the gap called out).
  4. Phase 3b, Dashboard Page 2: AnalyticsExample task: Build a salesperson leaderboard panel showing volume, total margin and average margin per deal side by side, so 'best' is never reduced to one number the viewer can't question.
