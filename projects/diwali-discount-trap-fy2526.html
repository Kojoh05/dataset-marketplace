What is messy in this dataset?

These are the problems you will find and fix before any analysis. They are put in on purpose, because real exports look like this.

  • About 190 orders pasted in twice with the same Order ID
  • Ten money columns in several text formats and five date formats including Excel serials
  • The month label blank on about 40% of rows
  • Each of 20 listing managers under about 8 name spellings; employee code blank on about 5%
  • Marketplaces and categories under about 5 spellings each; discount as 40%, 0.4000, 40.0 %
  • Order status under about 25 spellings

What business problem does this project solve?

A year of messy order exports from a fictional Indian appliance brand selling on Amazon, Flipkart, Meesho and its own site: 14,701 order lines, 41 columns, 20 listing managers, 40 SKUs. Clean it, then settle the argument management can't: why the biggest sales month lost money, which discount depth stops paying, and why the customers who paid full price were worth several times more than the ones who came in on sale.

Nivaas Home is a direct-to-consumer kitchen and home appliance brand based in Gurugram. It sells six product families (air fryers, mixer grinders, electric kettles, induction cooktops, vacuum cleaners and room heaters, forty SKUs in all) through four channels: Amazon.in, Flipkart, Meesho and its own website. Twenty listing managers each own one category on one channel: they set prices, pick discounts, run ads and answer for the numbers. Every order line lands in the company's order management export, keyed in or pasted together from four different marketplace dashboards.

The financial year just closed on 31 March 2026, and the founders are arguing about October.

The Sales Head is pointing at the revenue chart. For Diwali the company ran its biggest sale ever, 124.5 lakh of orders booked in October, 2.3 times September's 53.7 lakh. The listing manager who drove the most delivered sales that month and that year, Ritesh Rawat (Air Fryer, Amazon.in), is the obvious pick for the annual bonus. Nobody questions that October was the best month. It says so right there on the dashboard.

The Finance Head has done the arithmetic differently. After the cost of the goods, marketplace fees, shipping, packaging, ad spend and the cost of everything that came back, she says October did not make money. It lost money, in a year in which every other month did. And the person with the biggest sales number ranks near the bottom on the money they actually left in the business.

Nobody can agree what "best" means, and next Diwali is twelve months away. The founders have asked you to settle it before the plan for the next festive season is signed off.

The export is a year of order lines from four marketplaces, patched together by hand, and nothing agrees with anything.

What will you build, step by step?

  1. Phase 1, Python: Clean the DataExample task: Load the raw CSV/XLSX and profile it: row count, column count, null counts per column, and a written note on the five things that look most wrong before you touch anything.
  2. Phase 2, SQL: Answer the Business QuestionsExample task: Rank the 20 listing managers by delivered GMV (sum of order_value on Delivered lines). Return name, category, marketplace, GMV and rank.
  3. Phase 3a, Dashboard Page 1: Order LookupExample task: Build a search box that takes an Order ID and shows the full story of that one line: product and listing manager, the price chain (MRP -> discount -> selling price -> ex-GST revenue), the cost stack (COGS, fee, shipping, packaging, ad spend, return costs) and the resulting contribution as a waterfall.
  4. Phase 3b, Dashboard Page 2: AnalyticsExample task: Build a listing-manager leaderboard showing GMV, contribution and contribution margin % side by side, so 'best' is never reduced to one number the viewer can't question.
  5. Phase 4, DocumentationExample task: Write a short project write-up (any format: Word, PDF, Notion, a README) with three sections: (1) Process Flow: how you went from the raw export to cleaned data to SQL answers to your dashboard, including the decisions you made, especially how you resolved listing-manager and customer identity; (2) Key Discoveries: your top 3-5 findings, stated the way a decision-maker wants to hear them, not as code output; (3) Future Growth Recommendations: at least 3 concrete, data-backed recommendations for what Nivaas Home should do before next Diwali. This is the deliverable a hiring manager would actually read, so treat it that way.
