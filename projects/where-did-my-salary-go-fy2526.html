The story

Every first-jobber knows the feeling. Salary lands on the 1st, the month feels rich for a week, and by the 20th you are checking the balance before ordering dinner. Nobody can say where it went, because the money left in a hundred small UPI payments to names you do not recognise: BUNDL TECHNOLOGIES, ANI TECHNOLOGIES, KIRANAKART.

Paisa Diary, a small budgeting app, ran a twelve month pilot with 24 young professionals in Bengaluru, Gurugram, Noida, Pune, Hyderabad, Mumbai and Chennai. Each one shared their bank statements from April 2025 to March 2026. The app promised to categorise everything automatically. It did not: about a fifth of the rows have no category at all, and many of the rest are wrong.

The statements come from five banks, and every bank writes dates, amounts, balances and UPI narrations its own way. The monthly downloads overlap, so some transactions appear twice. Failed payments show up as a debit and a reversal. Some people really were charged twice. A few free trials quietly became paid subscriptions. And 8 of the 24 spent more than they earned across the year, kept afloat by money from home.

You are the analyst Paisa Diary hired to do what the app could not. Clean the statements, build categories you can trust, and tell each person where their salary went. Your license gives you one person and one month of your own to investigate in depth.

Questions

Is this real company data?

No. Every row is synthetic: the company, the people and the transactions are invented. The data is built to behave like real business data, including the mess, so the project feels like real work.

What tools do I need?

Python, SQL, Excel, Power BI work best, but the tools are your choice. What is checked is whether your answers are right.

How long does it take?

About 6 to 10 hours for most people, spread over a few evenings.

Can I put it on my portfolio?

Yes. Publish your analysis, code and dashboard on GitHub or LinkedIn. You can share up to 100 sample rows, but not the full files.
