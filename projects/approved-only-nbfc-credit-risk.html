The story

Suvidha Finance is a mid-sized NBFC that gives personal loans of ₹50,000 to ₹5 lakh to salaried and self-employed customers across India. Since January 2024 it has received 24,000 applications through its app, branches, website, fintech partners and a network of DSA agents. A simple rule decides: bureau score of 700 or more, total EMIs under 55% of income, income of at least ₹25,000. About half are approved.

Defaults are creeping up, the rule turns away good customers that competitors happily take, and the risk committee wants a proper scoring model and a cut-off backed by rupees.

The data looks complete, and it is not. Only approved applicants have a repayment history, so every model learns from people the old rule already liked. Loans from the last year have not had time to go bad, so recent months look deceptively safe. And the loan file carries columns such as collections agency and write-off amount that were written after the customer stopped paying.

Your job is to build a label that respects time, features that respect the application date, a model tested on loans it has never seen, and a cut-off that makes money at your own economics. Every license gets its own margin, loss given default and focus segment, so your break-even and your profit numbers are yours alone.
