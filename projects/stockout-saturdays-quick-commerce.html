The story

Zapkart delivers groceries in ten minutes from six dark stores in Bengaluru and Delhi NCR. Every store carries the same 40 fast moving items, restocked four mornings a week from the city warehouse. The replenishment rule is simple: top each SKU up to a fixed number of days of average sales.

The data says Saturday is a slow day. Store managers say the opposite: by Saturday afternoon the milk, the bread and the chips are gone, customers see "out of stock" and order from a rival. Both are right. Sales can never be higher than the stock on the shelf, so on a stockout day the export records what was sold, not what people wanted.

You have 92,900 rows of daily store and SKU data from January 2025 to February 2026, plus the SKU, store and festival calendars. One store opened only in August. Promos, IPL nights, Diwali and New Year's Eve all push demand around.

Your job is to estimate the demand the shelves hid, build a forecast that beats the current rule on February 2026, and turn it into an order quantity for Friday deliveries. Your license shares a brief with four other buyers: a store, a category and a service level target.
